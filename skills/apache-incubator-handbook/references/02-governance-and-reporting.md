# Governance, communication, reporting, and records

Last verified: 2026-08-02.

## Communication and decision records

- Public mailing lists are the primary record of project discussion and
  decisions. Chat, calls, GitHub comments, or private email may help work move,
  but material decisions should be summarized to the appropriate mailing list.
- Use the public `dev@` list for normal project governance, technical direction,
  releases, and community discussion.
- Use the private PPMC list only for confidential personnel, security, legal, or
  similarly sensitive matters. Move the non-confidential outcome to the public
  record when appropriate.
- ASF communities normally seek consensus. Lazy consensus is useful for
  ordinary reversible actions; formal votes are used when policy or durable
  governance evidence requires them.

## Voting vocabulary

- `+1`: approve.
- `0`: abstain or neutral.
- `-1`: oppose. For code changes it can be a veto when the applicable process
  uses consensus approval; release votes use majority approval and cannot be
  vetoed merely by writing `-1`.
- **Binding** means the voter has formal authority for that decision. The
  relevant body changes with the decision: PPMC, IPMC, or another ASF committee.
- Voting periods normally remain open at least 72 hours so volunteers across
  time zones can participate.

## Podling reports

- New podlings normally report monthly for their first three months, then move
  to their assigned quarterly group. A missed report can return a podling to
  monthly reporting.
- The IPMC chair sends the schedule and reminders. Treat the current reminder
  and current wiki template as live state; do not reuse an old template blindly.
- The PPMC is responsible for ensuring the report is prepared, but the whole
  community should be invited to contribute on the dev list.
- Mentors must sign off. Without Mentor sign-off, the report is not accepted and
  must be submitted again in the following month.

A useful report answers:

1. Does the IPMC or Board need to address anything?
2. Are there legal, infrastructure, cross-project, personal, or other risks?
3. What blocks the podling?
4. What happened since the previous report: releases, community additions,
   milestones, important discussions, and movement on earlier risks?
5. What will happen next?
6. Are Mentors active and providing help?

Prefer facts, counts, movement, and risks. Do not bury unresolved issues behind
general progress language.

## Status page and roster

- The podling status page tracks incubation tasks and important news. Keep it
  current, especially for releases, new committers/PPMC members, repositories,
  resources, grants, and completed incubation work.
- Maintain the authoritative podling roster through the current ASF roster
  tooling (commonly Whimsy). Do not infer membership from GitHub teams.
- Report archive URLs, vote results, grants, and other public evidence should
  be durable and discoverable rather than existing only in chat.

## Personnel decisions

- Discuss candidates on the private PPMC list.
- Follow the podling's documented voting practice and the current ASF account
  creation/roster procedure.
- Evaluate merit broadly: code is only one form of contribution. Documentation,
  review, support, release work, and community building count.
- Keep the bar understandable and avoid dependence on a single organization or
  closed nomination process.
