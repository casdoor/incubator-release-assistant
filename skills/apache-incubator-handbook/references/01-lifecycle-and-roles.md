# Podling lifecycle and roles

Last verified: 2026-08-02.

## Lifecycle in plain language

1. A candidate community prepares and discusses an Incubator proposal.
2. A Sponsor, Champion, and proposed Mentors help bring the proposal to the
   Incubator PMC (IPMC).
3. After acceptance, the podling receives ASF infrastructure, imports code with
   appropriate provenance, establishes public governance, reports progress,
   builds community, and learns to make compliant ASF releases.
4. A mature, independent podling may graduate as a top-level project (TLP), or
   may retire if the community cannot continue.

## Roles

- **ASF Board**: ultimately establishes TLPs and oversees PMCs. Podlings do not
  report directly to the Board; the IPMC provides oversight during incubation.
- **Incubator PMC (IPMC)**: the ASF committee responsible for the Incubator. It
  accepts podlings, oversees incubation, casts binding votes in second-phase
  podling release votes, and recommends graduation or ratifies retirement.
- **Sponsor**: the Apache entity backing the candidate and helping it through
  incubation. The Sponsor nominates Mentors and is expected to stay involved.
- **Champion**: helps a candidate prepare and navigate its proposal. The role is
  most visible before and during admission; it is not a substitute for Mentors.
- **Mentor**: an IPMC member assigned to guide and monitor a podling, connect it
  with IPMC/Infrastructure/Secretary processes, help with grants and policy,
  sign off reports, and assess whether the podling should continue, graduate,
  or retire. A project adviser is not automatically an ASF Mentor.
- **PPMC**: the Podling Project Management Committee. It oversees the podling,
  makes community and personnel decisions within delegated authority, reviews
  releases in the first phase, maintains reports/roster/status, and learns to
  operate like a future PMC.
- **Committer**: a person invited by the project with repository write access.
  Committership recognizes sustained merit; it is not the same as PPMC or IPMC
  membership and does not automatically make every vote binding.
- **Contributor**: anyone who contributes code, documentation, reviews,
  support, community work, or other value. Contribution is broader than commits.

## Podling versus TLP

- A TLP has its own PMC and reports to the Board.
- A podling has a PPMC and reports through the IPMC.
- The IPMC must formalize actions that a podling cannot yet make as an Act of
  the Foundation, most visibly final approval of ASF releases and graduation.
- Both should conduct normal work openly, grow meritocratically, and renew
  committers and committee membership.

## Operational shortcuts

- Ask “which role owns this decision?” before choosing a mailing list.
- Confirm Mentor and PPMC membership from official ASF roster sources rather
  than titles used in project chat or GitHub.
- Keep Mentors informed through public project channels; private communication
  is reserved for personnel, security, and other confidential matters.
